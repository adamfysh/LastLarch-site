ARIA-LEX: Structured Early Action Protocol Library

A flexible and dynamic seed library that gives sectors a basis upon which to design their Early Action protocols. All content is aligned with IFRC: PAPE Framework, WhatNow/AlertHub standards, WFP/OCHA anticipatory action protocols and sector best practice. It produces CAP-ready language for tailored early warnings.

Overview

A globally-grounded, credible starting point that national or sub-national authorities use as raw material to prepare their own early action protocols via adaptation workshops (per the IFRC/PAPE recommendation). Users come out of that process with their own approved action protocol library that is validated, politically endorsed and institutionally owned.

This is linked in ARIA, where ussers can draw or import polygons over affected areas and the system instantly retrieves demographic data, infrastructure information, and exposure statistics from pre-defined libraries. The data can be automatically connected via ARIA-Lex into pre-validated warning language that can be ported directly into Common Alerting Protocol (CAP).

Customized, CAP result is faster, more accurate warnings that help communities prepare for and respond to emergencies. What once took hours of manual data gathering and analysis can now happen in seconds.

What once took hours of manual data gathering can now happen in seconds. Warnings can be contextual, action-oriented and community-focused wihtout sacrificing warning speed.

The Challenge

Early Warnings are easy. Getting an observed phenomenon to trigger a loud noise is the simple part. But action is the hard part. Warnings that are too unspecific (a siren), to generic ("heavy rainfall will affect your area"), or too slow do not produce the kinds of actions that will save lives and assets. Some communities have built-in impact-based thresholds where, if the temperature or precipitation is anticipated to pass a threshold, some standard language is issued. But for people to really take action, the messages should be specific and clear and actionable. The Early Warning needs of a teacher caring for 30 young people are different from a fleet manager for a logistics firm or a safety operator at a power plant.

Technical Implementation

ARIA-Lex can be used as a standalone database and plugged into existing Early Action protocols or connected to ARIA to extract automated messages that are preapproved by the relevant authorities. All messages are by-default "unvalidated" and deactivated and intended only as a seed library for official validation. When seed messages are validated or edited and then validated, they can be easily activated and ported directly into existing protocols.

Register A Proactive Action Themes
ID Theme Description & Framework Mapping
A1 Situational awareness & monitoring Receiving, verifying, and tracking hazard information; monitoring thresholds; understanding the evolving risk picture. The "knowing" theme.
A2 Protective action — physical safety Evacuation, shelter-in-place, movement restrictions, securing structures. The core "get people out of harm's way" theme. Maps to IFRC PAPE's most fundamental messages.
A3 Resource & supply pre-positioning Stockpiling water, food, fuel, medicine, emergency kit items before impact. Pre-authorising release of supplies and funds. Prominent in WFP/FAO anticipatory action frameworks.
A4 Health & medical preparedness Pre-positioning medical supplies, activating surge capacity, medication continuity for dependent patients, infection prevention. WHO ERF and health cluster frameworks.
A5 Vulnerable population identification & outreach Proactively identifying and reaching elderly, disabled, pregnant, children, isolated, and other at-risk groups before impact. Strongly present in IFRC, UNICEF, and WHO frameworks.
A6 Safeguarding & duty of care Protection of children, staff, and beneficiaries from exploitation, separation, and harm in emergency settings. Primarily from UNICEF child protection, INEE, and sector codes of conduct.
A7 Mental health & psychosocial support (MHPSS) Activating MHPSS protocols, psychological first aid, staff wellbeing during prolonged events. WHO ERF and IASC MHPSS guidelines.
A8 Risk communication & public information Issuing clear, consistent, trusted warnings and instructions to the public and to sector audiences. Includes countering misinformation. IFRC RCCE and ITU communication frameworks.
Register B Operational & System Continuity
ID Theme Description & Technical Context
B1 Coordination & inter-agency activation Activating EOCs, ICS structures, cluster systems, mutual aid agreements, and inter-agency coordination mechanisms. The "who calls whom and in what order" theme.
B2 Infrastructure protection & hardening Securing physical assets before impact: reinforcing structures, moving vehicles and equipment, shutting down vulnerable systems, flood-proofing facilities.
B3 Service continuity & backup activation Activating backup systems, redundant pathways, alternate sites, and continuity-of-operations plans. Focuses on keeping functions running when primary systems fail.
B4 Network & operational rerouting Actively rerouting traffic, supply chains, transport networks, or data flows away from the hazard area (e.g., road diversions, telecom traffic shifting).
B5 Workforce & personnel management Staff safety, shift scheduling for emergencies, evacuation of non-essential personnel, surge roster activation, and field worker accountability.
B6 Financial & legal pre-authorisation Unlocking emergency funding, pre-authorising procurement, activating insurance, and releasing contingency budgets. Key for trigger-based financing.
B7 Data, records & documentation protection Backing up critical data, securing student/patient/operational records, and protecting legal documents across sectors.
B8 Livelihoods & asset protection Protecting productive assets before impact: livestock, crops, fishing equipment, and small business assets. Central to FAO and WFP frameworks.
B9 Regulatory & policy emergency powers Invoking emergency legislation, issuing directives, and activating international emergency frameworks (IHR, Tampere Convention, ICAO).
